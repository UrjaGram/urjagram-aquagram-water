[10:54 pm] **Varad Vyas:** Katlabodi Survey 

1. Socio-Economic & Administrative Baseline
Demographics: 75% of the population is in agriculture; 25% in industrial/other jobs. Predominantly Adivasi (tribal) community.

Income: Average household income is ~₹50,000/year. Average salary is ~₹15,000.

Government Schemes: 0% penetration across all sectors (water, crops, seeds). subsidies exist, but villagers have absolute zero awareness of them.

Healthcare: 0 hospitals for humans or animals. The village relies entirely on calling private doctors from nearby locations.

Connectivity: Zero internet or cellular tower coverage. The BBNL Wi-Fi in the Gram Panchayat office is non-functional. However, every household owns one smartphone.

2. Agricultural & Market Dynamics
Major Crops: Wheat, Cotton, Tur Dal, Chana, and Jawari.

Market Linkages: Farmers sell all crops and daily milk (60-70 liters) exclusively in local markets. They avoid Nagpur mandis because transportation costs negate profits.

Agricultural Knowledge: Zero awareness regarding optimal seeds and fertilizers. They rely entirely on unverified advice from local vendors.

Fodder Management: Animals graze freely in nearby forests during monsoon and winter. In summer, they grow fodder or buy maize/kod from the market (approximate summer fodder cost: ₹1 Lakh).

Crop Failure Triggers: Erratic rain (heavy or low) and wildlife damage. A Sarpanch's mango tree plantation initiative damage due to monkey attacks.

Soil Types: Black, Red, Bhasma, and White, yellow soil. (local mine previously existed).

3. Water Resources & Energy Bottlenecks
Water Source: 100% groundwater dependent (separate borewells for the village and fields). The water table is at 40-60 feet. (Locals believe an underground stream feeds it).

Rainwater Harvesting: 0%. There are no check dams, rivers, ponds, or lakes.

Drinking Water: Tested monthly. If impurities increase, purifying packets are added to the main tank.

Electricity: 24/7 supply for the village. Agricultural supply is 8 hours/day in shifts.

Solar Subsidy Failure: The government mandates solar for new farm connections Reasons: Farmers cannot afford the 25% upfront cost, the paperwork process is too complex, and solar voltage is insufficient to run heavy agricultural pumps for 8 continuous hours.

4. Untapped Potential (Field Observations)
Adoption Mindset: Villagers are highly receptive to adopting new crops or methods, provided there is a clear and sufficient ROI.

Women's Labor Force: Local women are willing to dedicate 4-5 hours daily to micro-industries (like agarbatti manufacturing), which they have done in the past.

Bio-Resources: A population of ~1,000 livestock (cows, buffaloes, goats) generates a minimum of 150 tractor-loads of dung annually.
